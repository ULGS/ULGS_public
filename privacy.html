Privacy Policy

ULTRALIGHT GEOSPATIAL LLC · EFFECTIVE [[SET-AT-DEPLOY]]

Ultralight Geospatial LLC ("ULGS," "we," "us") builds offline-first field tools. This page describes what our mobile apps and this website do with information, and the choices you have. It applies to every version of every app we publish — free, limited, and paid alike.

THE SHORT VERSION: We operate no servers — nothing you record is collected or stored by us. No accounts. No ads, no trackers. Your records are stored on your device, never with us. Location on your photos is your choice. The full policy below explains each of these.

Our Apps

This section applies to every mobile application published by Ultralight Geospatial LLC. Where apps differ, the difference is stated here by name. If an app is ever governed by a separate policy instead, it will be named here, and that app's store listing will link to its own policy — the policy linked from an app's store listing is always the one that governs that app. As of the effective date above, no such app exists: this page is the complete privacy policy for every ULGS app. Our current apps are ULGS Nav (navigation), R What? (canyon elevation profiles), and TelCam (field camera); where behavior differs by app, this page names the app. Apps are currently distributed in the United States (testing phase).

Location

Recording your device's GPS position — points, tracks, photo positions, elevation profiles — is what these tools are for. Location is used only while you are actively using an app to record or navigate, or while a recording you started is running. In ULGS Nav, a recording you start continues while your screen is off or locked, shown by a visible ongoing notification; stopping the recording ends it. The same service also runs, under a different notification, while the app is simply open showing your position — stopping a recording ends the recording; leaving the app is what ends location delivery. R What? has no background recording at all: it works only while it is open on screen. Position data is stored locally in the app's private storage.

Position fixes and Google's network location. On standard Android, if an app is allowed to use network-based location, the operating system may resolve your position through Google's network location service — meaning nearby cell and Wi-Fi information leaves your device to Google, by the platform, not by our code. Where our apps offer a GPS-only mode, choosing it means the app requests satellite positioning only; where network location is used at all, it is as a fallback when GPS has no fix. On de-Googled Android systems this platform service behaves according to that system's own configuration.

Your records

Waypoints, tracks, logs, routes, notes, and photos you create are stored on your device, never with us. We cannot see, access, retrieve, or delete them. Where a feature sends route geometry to a public service, the services section above says so, plainly. What happens to your records if you uninstall an app depends on where that app keeps them:

  • Tracks, routes, logs, and settings (all apps): removed when you uninstall the app. Records you have not exported are gone with it.
  • Files you exported, and map sheets you downloaded (downloads are ULGS Nav's): yours, saved to your device's shared storage (such as Downloads), and they remain after uninstall — the app no longer owns them; you do.
  • Photos (TelCam): saved to your device's photo gallery, and they remain after uninstall like any other photos. Manage or delete them in your gallery.

Device backup and phone-to-phone transfer are governed by your device's own backup and migration settings.

Platforms, today and later

Our apps currently run on Android phones and tablets, and this policy describes that architecture. We are interested in other form factors — watches, vehicle systems, and sharing between nearby devices. If we ship for them, some involve pathways this policy doesn't yet describe: syncing between a phone and a watch, for example, can pass through the platform's own services, the way network location does today. Our commitment is the same one this whole page rests on: any new data pathway will be described here, by name, before the version that uses it is released — and the promises that don't depend on architecture (no ULGS servers, no collection or storage by us, no selling, no ads, no accounts) hold on every platform we ever ship for.

Online map & elevation services

Some of our apps can fetch public map and terrain data over the network. When they do, the request goes from your device to the public service, under that service's own privacy policy — no ULGS system is anywhere in the path, and we receive nothing. Like many large services, some are delivered through a content-delivery network: requests to USGS 3DEP travel via Amazon CloudFront, whose edge servers carry that traffic under Amazon's own policies. One of our apps opts out entirely: TelCam declares no INTERNET permission and cannot contact any service — a structural property of the app, not a setting. The services our apps contact, by name:

  • USGS 3DEP (elevation.nationalmap.gov) — elevation samples and terrain imagery. Used by ULGS Nav — and it is the only service R What? contacts: two request paths to this one host, nothing else, and both refuse cross-host redirects, so the app cannot be steered to another server.
  • Oregon DOGAMI (gis.dogami.oregon.gov) and Washington DNR (gis.dnr.wa.gov) — state lidar and terrain layers (ULGS Nav)
  • OpenStreetMap Overpass (overpass-api.de) — map feature data (ULGS Nav)
  • USGS / USFS — downloadable map sheets (ULGS Nav): the map-sheet catalog at tnmaccess.nationalmap.gov. The download itself follows the link the catalog's reply supplies — data.fs.usda.gov for USFS sheets, and currently prd-tnm.s3.amazonaws.com (Amazon infrastructure) for USGS sheets — so the download host is chosen by the federal service, not fixed by our code

Be aware of what these requests contain. Fetching a map sheet or terrain layer sends the area you are asking about — the loaded sheet's own published extent, not where on it you are looking. Sampling elevation along a route sends coordinates that trace that route: in ULGS Nav, enriching a track's elevations sends the track itself, and opening a saved route's elevation profile automatically sends about a hundred points of that route to the OpenStreetMap Overpass service to name nearby landmarks — once per route, with no button pressed; in R What?, opening or importing a track automatically sends the derived sampling corridor around your line, which can run to thousands of points. One map-search mode in ULGS Nav can send your current GPS position as the search center — the only request in any of our apps that carries your live position. Nothing about you — no name, account, or identifier we control — accompanies these requests, and we store nothing; but each request necessarily tells that service the line you are tracing or the area you loaded, and their servers keep ordinary logs. In R What?, your live GPS position is never stored and never transmitted — held in memory to draw your dot while the app is open, it goes nowhere else.

Fetched data is cached in the app's private storage (clearable in the app, removed with the app at uninstall) so areas you have loaded keep working offline. A cache is also, by its nature, a record: its files are named for the areas you fetched, and clearing the cache is how you clear that record. If you prefer that no request leave your device in the field, load your maps and terrain at home and work from the cache — the apps are built for exactly that, with the one exception above: the first time you open a route's profile, the landmark fetch fires if that route has none stored.

Photos

In TelCam, location embedding is under your control, and photos can carry no location at all. Beyond that, the two copies the app can produce are deliberately separate in what they carry: the located copy contains position data and no camera metadata; the No Mark copy contains the camera's ordinary technical metadata — for example make, model, firmware version, and exposure settings — and no location data. No file the app produces contains both a position and a device description. Neither copy contains serial numbers or any per-device identifier.

Crash reports

If an app offers a crash report, the report is a file written on your device. It contains technical state — not your records or location history — and it is shown to you in full. Nothing sends automatically, in any app. In TelCam, sending is your action, by an email you compose; in ULGS Nav, the report is saved to your Downloads by your action in the app, and sharing it — by any means, or not at all — is entirely up to you. Anything you type into an optional note is included as you wrote it, so describe the problem in whatever detail you're comfortable sharing.

When we diagnose a report, the technical contents describe our own code, and we analyze them with our development tools. Your email address and anything you wrote in the note are yours: we separate them from the technical contents before analysis, and we never feed them into any tool or service that is permitted to train on them.

Permissions

Each app requests only what its own features require, read from the apps' merged manifests. TelCam declares Camera and Location. R What? declares Location only. ULGS Nav declares seven: fine and coarse location (positioning; coarse is the required network-provider companion), internet (the map and terrain fetches above), foreground service and its location type (recording that continues with the screen off), notifications (the ongoing recording status), and wake lock (keeps a recording alive while the screen is off) — seven that we declare, plus one entry AndroidX defines for its own internal receivers, which grants nothing to anyone and is not something we request. No app requests a storage or media permission. File import opens the system picker, which grants access to exactly the files you choose. File export writes to your shared Downloads with no system prompt: the file lands where other software can read it and remains after uninstall — exporting is the act that makes it shared. TelCam writes photos to your gallery through the platform's media store — it can reach the photos it created, not the rest of your gallery. Network access is described in the services section above, and TelCam has none. Decline or revoke any runtime permission in your device settings; the corresponding feature stops and the rest of the app carries on.

Purchases, free versions, and paid versions

Paid licenses and upgrades are processed by Google Play. We receive transaction records (what was purchased, when, in what country) but never your payment card details.

Where we offer a free or limited version of an app, this same policy applies without exception: no advertising, no trackers, no accounts, and no additional data practices of any kind. A free version is a smaller tool, not a data funnel. Our apps do not phone home to check licenses, and we do not restrict your ability to export your own data in any version.

This Website

ulgeospatial.com is informational. No accounts, no advertising, no analytics scripts, no cookies set by us.

  • Server logs. Our hosting provider records standard access logs (IP address, browser type, pages requested) to operate and secure the site.
  • Fonts. Pages load a typeface from Google Fonts, which means your browser requests a file from Google's servers; Google's privacy policy governs that request.

Children

Our apps and site are professional field tools intended for adults. They are not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has provided us personal information, contact support@ulgeospatial.com and we will delete it.

Your Rights

Because your records live on your device and not on our servers, you exercise most rights directly: view, export, and delete your data in the app at any time.

For what little we do hold — support email and store transaction records — you may request access, correction, or deletion at support@ulgeospatial.com. Residents of California, the EU/EEA, the UK, and other jurisdictions with privacy statutes may have additional rights under those laws; we honor valid requests regardless of where you live, and we do not discriminate against anyone for exercising a privacy right.

Security

Your records sit in app-private storage (or, for photos and exports, your device's own gallery and Downloads), protected by your device's security. Because we do not hold your data, the measures that matter are on your device: use device encryption and a strong screen lock, and follow your organization's device policies where they apply.
