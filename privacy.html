Privacy Policy

ULTRALIGHT GEOSPATIAL LLC · EFFECTIVE [[SET-AT-DEPLOY]]

Ultralight Geospatial LLC ("ULGS," "we," "us") is the data controller for this policy. Address and contact are in the footer. This page covers our mobile apps and this website, and applies to every version of every app we publish.

Our apps are ULGS Nav (navigation), R What? (canyon elevation profiles), and TelCam (field camera) — Android applications, distributed through Google Play in the United States.

// 01

Our apps

  • No app asks you to register, sign in, or create a profile; using an app puts no record of you in our hands.
  • No ULGS servers. We operate no service any app contacts. Nothing you create is transmitted to us, and no app checks a license with us.
  • No third-party advertising, analytics, or crash-reporting SDKs in any version. Where an app has a crash reporter it is our own, it writes to your device, and it sends nothing on its own — see each app below.
  • No sale or sharing of personal information, as those terms are used in California law. We hold no user records to sell.
  • We do not use your personal information to train machine-learning models, and we do not sell or license it to anyone for that purpose. Anything you send us — a crash report, a photograph or file for testing or support, or material for commissioned work — is used only for what you sent it for.
  • Export is not restricted in any version of any app.
  • Permissions may be declined or revoked in your device settings; the corresponding feature stops and the rest of the app continues.

Your device's location. Your device fixes its position either from satellites (GNSS) or from a network-based service that estimates position from nearby cell towers and Wi-Fi. On most Android phones that network service is Google's, part of Google Play services, and using it sends those nearby signals to Google under Google's terms; on a device without Google's services it is whatever the manufacturer or system provides, or nothing at all. Either way it is your device doing the positioning, not our code. Our apps are built to work from satellite positioning alone, and where an app offers a positioning-source control, choosing satellites-only stops the app requesting network positions rather than merely ignoring them — ULGS Nav has such a control.

Third-party recipients. Where an app fetches public map or terrain data, the request goes from your device to that public service under its own privacy policy; no ULGS system is in the path. Some of those services reach you through third-party infrastructure: content delivery networks that carry the traffic, and storage hosts that serve the files. We neither control nor observe those operators' logs.

AI and machine learning. We use software tools, including AI-assisted analysis, on our own engineering and testing data, on public datasets, and on our own source code. We do not knowingly submit anything you send us to a service that trains on it. What we cannot promise is control of the platforms we run the business on — email, storage, developer tools — each of which applies its own terms to what passes through it, including AI features we neither operate nor audit — our email provider, named in section 04, is one example. We send no more than a task needs and use the settings and account types that restrict such use where they are offered, but this area is changing quickly and we would rather state the limit than imply a guarantee we cannot give.

New data pathways will be described on this page before the app version that uses them is released.

Each app

Select an app.

ULGS NAV navigation · contacts public map and terrain services

Permissions. Location, network access, notifications, and the foreground-service permissions that let a recording continue while the screen is off. One further permission is declared but used by no current feature, and one manifest entry is defined by a Google support library for its own internal use rather than requested by us. No storage or media permission. The exact declaration list for a given release is in the technical inventory described in section 09.

Network. The app contacts USGS (elevation service and map-sheet catalog), Oregon DOGAMI, Washington DNR, the USDA Forest Service, and the OpenStreetMap Overpass project. Requests carry the map area being loaded or the coordinates of the route being processed; no name, account, or identifier we control accompanies them. Two behaviors are worth stating plainly: opening a saved route's elevation profile automatically sends points from that route to the Overpass service, once per route, without a separate action by you; and one map-search mode sends your current position as the search center. Map-sheet downloads follow a download link supplied by the federal catalog's reply, so the host serving a download is chosen by that service rather than fixed by our code.

Location. A recording you start continues while the screen is off or locked, shown by an ongoing notification. Stopping the recording ends the recording; leaving the app ends location delivery. A GPS-only mode is available and requests satellite positioning only.

On your device. Tracks, routes, waypoints, settings, and fetched map and terrain caches are held in app-private storage and removed when you uninstall. Cached files are named for the areas fetched, so the cache is a record of where you loaded data; it can be cleared in the app. Exported files and downloaded map sheets are written to shared storage such as Downloads: they are readable by other software, they remain after uninstall, and exports carry geometry, your labels, and creation times.

Backup and transfer. Cloud backup is disabled. App data is included in phone-to-phone migration, so moving to a new handset does not destroy unexported records.

Diagnostics. A crash report is a file written on your device and shown to you in full. Nothing is sent automatically. Saving it to Downloads is your action, and sharing it afterward is entirely yours. Text you type into the optional note is included as written.

R WHAT? canyon elevation profiles · contacts one elevation service

Permissions. Location only. No storage or media permission, no background location, and no foreground service.

Network. The app contacts one service: the USGS 3DEP elevation service. Every time you open or import a track — whether or not it already has elevations — the app automatically sends a set of coordinates derived from that line, which may be several thousand points, and stores the returned elevations with the track. No name, account, or identifier we control accompanies the request.

Location. Your live position is used only while the app is open, to show your position against the profile. It is never stored and never transmitted.

On your device. Profiles, elevations, and cached terrain are held in app-private storage and removed when you uninstall. Files you export are written to shared storage and remain after uninstall. They contain route geometry and elevations — no live position and no timestamps — and any name or description you typed onto a waypoint, as you wrote it.

Backup and transfer. Cloud backup is disabled. App data is included in phone-to-phone migration.

Diagnostics. The app has no crash-reporting mechanism.

TELCAM field camera · no network access

Permissions. Camera and location. No storage or media permission.

Network. TelCam declares no internet permission and cannot contact any service. It sends nothing, to us or to anyone.

Location. TelCam records device telemetry onto and into photographs. You can toggle HUD/Watermark elements, and you can have the app save a second copy with neither telemetry drawn on it nor location data in its EXIF. That second copy is off unless you turn it on. If your device has no position fix, no location is written and the image is marked NO FIX where the position would otherwise appear.

Photographs. With the second copy turned on, the app saves two files. The located copy contains position data, the file's basic image and timestamp fields, and a TelCam version stamp, and no description of your device. The No Mark copy contains the camera's ordinary technical metadata — for example make, model, firmware version, and exposure settings — and no location data. No file the app produces contains both a position and a device description, and neither copy contains serial numbers or any per-device identifier. Photographs are written to your device's photo gallery through the platform's media store; the app can reach the photos it created, not the rest of your gallery, and photographs remain in your gallery after uninstall.

On your device. Settings, diagnostic records, and imported boundary files are held in app-private storage and removed when you uninstall. A boundary file supplied to you by email is checked against a single key built into the app before it is read, and a file failing that check is refused. That check proves the file came from us and has not been altered since; it is the same key in every copy of the app and identifies neither you nor your device. No network is involved. Diagnostic records you choose to save to Downloads are in shared storage and remain after uninstall.

Backup and transfer. Cloud backup and phone-to-phone transfer are both disabled for this app's own data.

Diagnostics. A crash report is a file written on your device and shown to you in full. Nothing is sent automatically; sending is your action, by an email you compose in your own mail app. Text you type into the optional note is included as written.

If you send us a crash report, a photograph, or a file — to diagnose a problem, or as a tester helping us check what an app writes into a file — we use it only for that purpose. We separate your address and anything you wrote from the technical contents before analysis, we do not publish or pass on what you send, and we delete it on request. Files sent for testing can carry position data and whatever the photograph shows, so send only what you are willing to share.

Retention and deletion

We hold no records of yours to retain or delete: no account, no server, no copy of anything you create. We hold only Google Play transaction records, kept by Google under its terms and visible to us as the seller, and email correspondence, kept as long as needed to answer you and deleted on request. If you commission custom work from us, section 10 describes what we hold and for how long.

Everything else is on your device. Section 02 states, for each app, what uninstall removes and what remains for you to manage — photographs, exported files, and downloaded map sheets are in your device's own storage and are yours to delete there.

Your rights

Because your records are on your device, you exercise most rights directly: view, export, and delete your data in the app at any time. Export also serves portability — the formats are open and readable by other software.

For the little we do hold — correspondence, material for commissioned work, and store transaction records — you may request access, correction, deletion, a copy, restriction of how we use it, or object to our use of it, at support@ulgeospatial.com. We keep correspondence to answer you, commissioned material to do and support the work you asked for, and invoices because tax law requires it. We operate from the United States, so anything you send us is processed there.

Where the GDPR or UK GDPR applies to you, those are our legal bases — our agreement with you, our legitimate interest in supporting our software, and legal obligation for tax records — and you may also lodge a complaint with your national data protection authority. We honor valid requests regardless of where you live, and we do not discriminate against anyone for exercising a privacy right.

Custom and commissioned work

We prepare custom geospatial material on request — for example a boundary file built for a customer and delivered by email for import into TelCam. This is a service engagement, and it is the one circumstance in which we hold material of yours.

What we receive. Your contact and billing details, and whatever source material you send us: coordinates, shapefiles, maps, or a description of the area to be drawn.

What we do with it. We use it to produce and support your deliverable. We do not publish it, share it with anyone outside ULGS, include it in data bundled with any app, or use it to train machine-learning models. Where the area is sensitive — private property, culturally significant sites, or restricted or operational areas — we treat it as confidential and do not redistribute it in any form.

How long we keep it. Source material and deliverables are kept while we support the work and deleted on your request; ask at support@ulgeospatial.com. Invoices and correspondence are retained as long as accounting and tax rules require, which we cannot delete on request.

Once a boundary file is on your device it is yours: the app reads it locally, sends nothing, and removes its copy when you uninstall.
