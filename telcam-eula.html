Where the readings come from, and what affects them

TelCam records what your device reports. It does not measure position, heading, or elevation itself, and it does not improve what your device's sensors produce.

Readings can be wrong or imprecise. What your device reports is affected by, among other things:

  1. the sensor hardware in your device and its condition;
  2. satellite visibility and geometry;
  3. terrain, tree canopy, buildings, vehicles, and other obstructions;
  4. magnetic interference and the calibration state of the compass;
  5. whether network-assisted positioning is available and enabled, and what it has to work with;
  6. your device's date, time, and location settings;
  7. how the device is held and moved at the moment of capture.

Positions may come from satellites alone or, if you enable network-assisted positioning, from your device's own positioning service, which estimates location from nearby cell towers and Wi-Fi. Either way, the positioning is done by your device, not by TelCam. Other apps on your device may use different positioning sources or processing, and may show a position when TelCam does not, or a different one.

TelCam displays the accuracy figures your device's sensors report; marks a photograph NO FIX when no position is available rather than writing a position it does not have; and marks photographs whose position relied on network estimation. Displayed precision follows measured accuracy. None of this is a guarantee.

Boundary and map outlines. Boundary files come from public datasets, from data we create, or from data you supply. Where we have processed public data, the modifications are ours, and the agencies named as sources neither performed nor endorsed them. Outlines are for general reference. They are not a survey, and they do not establish any legal boundary or land ownership.
